A little bit about me

Grounded in trauma-informed, attachment-focused care

  • EMDR
  • IFS
  • Psychodrama
  • Experiential Therapy

My clinical background spans a wide range of settings, including inpatient and outpatient treatment for substance use, eating disorders, and complex mental health challenges. These experiences have given me a deep understanding of how trauma and early life experiences shape the way we see ourselves, our relationships, and the world around us.

Every person has an inherent need to feel seen, heard, and understood. When these critical needs aren't met, it's common for coping mechanisms that are both ineffective and self-destructive to take shape, which can then lead to feelings of frustration, isolation, and a sense of being “stuck.” Whether your negative feelings manifest as anxiety, depression, impulsive behaviors, addiction, or the inability to connect with others, navigating these struggles alone can be overwhelming.

No matter how helpless you may feel, it's important to know that you're not alone. Finding healing and freedom is possible. I passionately help clients navigate relational trauma and attachment wounds that show up individually and in relationships as anxiety, depression, disordered eating, substance use, and other unwanted behaviors.

In my private practice, I specialize in helping clients heal from attachment wounds and trauma rooted in early relationships with primary caregivers. I integrate EMDR and experiential therapy into my work, and I continue expanding my training with IFS to offer even deeper avenues of healing. It is a privilege to assist clients in identifying and challenging the thoughts and behaviors that are blocking them from becoming their true selves.

Healing is not about fixing what is broken. It is about reclaiming your wholeness, finding freedom from old patterns, and building a life rooted in connection, hope, and authenticity.

Recommended reading

Books I often recommend to clients

A short shelf of books that show up again and again in session, alongside a few honest notes on why I return to each one.

The Body Keeps the Score
Bessel van der Kolk

The book I recommend most often. It explains, in plain language, why trauma lives in the body and not just the mind, and it helps clients understand that their symptoms make sense given what they've been through.

No Bad Parts
Richard Schwartz

A gentle introduction to Internal Family Systems, the model I use most in individual work. It reframes self-criticism as a protective part rather than a flaw, which tends to be a relief for clients who are exhausted by self-judgment.

Attached
Amir Levine and Rachel Heller

A clear, research-based look at attachment styles and how they shape our closest relationships. Couples I work with often read this together, and it gives them shared language for patterns they've noticed but couldn't quite name.

Adult Children of Emotionally Immature Parents
Lindsay C. Gibson

Useful for clients working through attachment wounds from childhood. It names dynamics many people have felt for years but never had words for, and it does so without placing blame.
